## What a better programme must demonstrate

1. **Useful specific work.** Preserve requested objectives, familiar exercise purposes, weekly opportunities and event identities when feasible. Distinguish heavy, productive rep work and speed/technique sets; low-effort doubles are not equivalent working volume. No universal set floor or compulsory single is imposed. A running-priority novice requesting strength progression must have a coherent progression/review route, rather than permanent entry loading or maintenance by accident.
2. **Evidence-calibrated starting suggestions.** Relevant, completed, dated, compatible performance should inform familiar working suggestions when it is more informative than a declared maximum. Explain uncertainty, role, reps, execution and effort. A historical maximum is not a floor during deterioration. Incompatible setup, failure, warm-ups, optional work, future dates, duplicates and implausible/contradictory evidence cannot establish improved capacity. Repeating a familiar load is not proof of adaptation. A product evidence horizon is not a biological expiration date.
3. **Approximate effort is limited evidence.** Preserve the original logged RPE and any retrospective review separately. An approximate speed-session RPE can support a familiar practice reference; it cannot establish a maximum, competition-single readiness, measured velocity or current recovery. No owner's proposed kilograms are a production target or an acceptance criterion.
4. **Progression with context and consent.** Distinct comparable planned opportunities across an actual programme cycle can support discussion of progression. Dense logs, multiple sets in one visit and a single easy session cannot establish a sustained trend. A positive control must show that genuine spaced evidence can still produce useful advice. Session execution adjustments remain possible immediately; material programme changes remain proposals requiring approval. No regeneration, accepted decision, history rewrite or activation occurs during this assessment.
5. **One coherent recovery interpretation.** Dated easy-running tolerance, quality/long-running readiness, lower-lifting readiness and upper-lifting tolerance are different evidence. An older normal report cannot override newer worsening; future, undated or pre-event reports cannot clear a completed/future event. Conflicting scopes require clarification. Persistent negative evidence cannot be cleared by calendar expiry. Missing information cannot justify either unrestricted habitual work or a permanent event-only prohibition. Any bounded familiar practice is provisional, task-specific and accompanied by a reason and reassessment point; it is not full clearance. Pain/altered movement requires stopping the provoking task and a safety review, not a percentage-only workaround.
6. **Recovery dose has dimensions.** Restoring intensity, set count, frequency, run quality and habitual mileage are separate decisions. One completed lower session or comfortable easy run does not restore all of them. A low recovery-week running total is not a newly measured capacity and cannot become an unexplained indefinite ceiling. Recovery cases must include fast, slow, uncertain, partial and worsening trajectories, including novice lifting.
7. **Competition preparation.** Retain familiar specific practice and a sensible fatigue reduction. Proximity alone does not justify near-max singles, new competition execution or an RPE-9 target. Consider actual single experience, recent relevant heavy practice, current fatigue and event priority. Avoid late catch-up work and preserve event/taper identities. An easy day is not automatically undertraining. A lower final single may be a reasonable tradeoff, not a proven optimum.
8. **Running and concurrent training.** Keep paces tied to demonstrated current fitness with explicit uncertainty, not aspirational goal alone. No universal 80/20 distribution, 10% safe increase or fixed post-marathon clearance is claimed. Generated executable duration, steps and distance must agree. Preserve useful easy/quality work when feasible; overlap alone is not evidence that concurrent training is harmful. Cuts require an interpretable recovery, performance, scheduling or priority rationale. No running data are invented for the owner.
9. **Stability and preservation.** Single-session feedback primarily informs execution/monitoring; weekly reflection examines patterns; approximately 2–4 weeks can be a practical review horizon, interpreted alongside performance, adherence and recovery rather than a mandatory wait for safety or a validated threshold. Existing approved/frozen prescriptions keep their policy until an explicitly approved replacement. Original logs/RPE, completed sessions, Garmin associations and existing approval/rollback semantics remain intact.
