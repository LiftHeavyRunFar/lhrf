## Stability, practical recovery and remaining limits

The new optional intake controls record activity, completed date, subsequent response date and status at the proposal boundary. They retain prior concerns and require matching actual completed activity; same-day readiness is not a next-day response. Unknown remains unknown. A later adverse report can invalidate older normal evidence. This is a usable route to reassessment, not an automatic regeneration or history edit.

A normal response can justify repeating a compatible observed working dose, including familiar fives. It does not justify extra sets, reps or intensity, and it need not require a heavy single/triple merely to repeat fives. A new universal RPE-6.5 ceiling on corroborated normal work would also undertrain athletes. Further progression requires recurring objective performance, adherence and recovery context, plus athlete approval. Single-session feedback informs execution/monitoring; weekly reflection identifies patterns; approximately 2–4 weeks is a practical normal-review horizon, not a biological cutoff or a reason to postpone safety action.

The engine still uses effort curves, load rounding, evidence horizons, intensity budgets, planned reduced weeks and provisional return fractions. These are explicit conventions, not empirical safety thresholds. They can exclude useful ambiguous/legacy evidence or underdose a recovered athlete until clarification. Exact setup/effort uncertainty must be explained rather than silently interpreting zero kilograms as inability. A variation without comparable completed evidence remains uncalibrated; a return plan should not call it proven familiar or safe. Significant pain requires assessment; a percentage-only adjustment cannot establish safety.

Neither preserved volume nor less reactive adaptation proves an individual programme optimal. A's first week still has 50 total sets including optional/support work; C has 46. A runner learning strength now has more relevant working loads and potentially more fatigue than the old low suggestions. Warm-ups and actual effort still govern, and symptoms/global fatigue can justify a different arrangement. The scientific review does not prove every lighter session is undertraining or every retained session harmless.
