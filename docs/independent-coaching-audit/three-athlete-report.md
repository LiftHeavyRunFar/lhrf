# Three-athlete challenge

All athletes are fictional. Inputs were declared before generation in [the fixture](../../tests/fixtures/independent-coaching-inputs.cjs). Reproduce with `node scripts/independent-coaching-audit.cjs`. [Complete session prescriptions](generated-programmes.md) include every generated exercise, set, rep, kg suggestion, RPE, run segment and pace for all 32 athlete-weeks. [Machine evidence](three-athlete-evidence.json) preserves exact input, complete output, policy markers, source hashes, synthetic completed records and review decisions. No programme was installed, replaced or activated. In the completion stress scenario all optional rows were performed; optional work is not necessary baseline workload.

The block clock is 7 November 2026, with a 9 November start. This is a forward preview, not a claim that a result remains current throughout the block. Completion replays advance the generation clock to each week's start and the review clock to its end. Reviewed snapshots in the app can remain frozen instead; their behavior is not inferred from these generator replays. Repeated weekly reviews in one athlete do not constitute independent athletes or a clinical efficacy trial.

Generous 120–180 minute windows reduce time scarcity in the initial weeks; actual first-week estimates are below the caps. C nevertheless loses optional work in later weeks, as detailed below. These athletes are contrasts selected for audit coverage, not a randomized or representative population. A weight of 0 in machine output means an unconfirmed setup-specific suggestion requiring calibration; it is not a prescription to lift no weight. “Main sets” includes any generated primary exercise/variation; speed/technique sets are identified separately. Hard work-distance share means prescribed work km divided by non-race training km, not measured physiological zone time.

## Athlete A — strength-priority hybrid

Advanced lifter; declared 155 kg bench maximum, recent competition-paused singles 145 kg @8.5 and 147.5 kg @9, modest 18 km/week running and 50-minute 10K. Bench meet 12 December (week 5); 10K 20 December (week 6), 47-minute aspiration. Requested three bench, one squat, one deadlift and two run exposures. Bench roles are explicitly heavy/volume/speed. Other lifts default to maintenance for the bench-only meet; that is an engine choice to review with the athlete, rather than an unspoken physiological necessity.

First-week calendar is Monday squat+heavy bench, Tuesday race-pace repeats, Wednesday deadlift+volume bench, Thursday speed bench, Sunday long easy run. The two consecutive bench visits Wednesday/Thursday are visible; the latter is low-effort speed practice. First-week inclusive visit estimates total 368 minutes, including running; the largest lifting visit is 98 minutes. Both run sessions are 9 km. All weeks are feasible with these inputs and no hard-limit removals.

Bench work in weeks 1–3 is one heavy single plus two triples, one five-rep top plus two five-rep back-offs, and five speed doubles: 11 bench sets, of which six are RPE 7+ and five are speed sets at 5.5. Count these stimuli separately. Suggested single 142.5→145→145 kg @8→8.5→8.5 follows the recent evidence. A's recent calibrated baseline is an estimate, not another tested maximum. Speed follows the fixed wave and has no measured velocity. The final heavy practice is Monday 7 December, 147.5 kg @9, five days before the meet; optional earned increments are metadata for review, not automatically applied loads.

Working lower-body maintenance is two triples each for squat/deadlift at RPE 7.5 in early weeks. This is meaningful practice, though a small hypertrophy/development dose. Adding a heavy single to either lift merely to satisfy a generic hybrid checklist can add fatigue without clear relevance to A's primary bench goal. Support volume drops sharply as the meet approaches: all strength sets 50 in week 1, 29 in weeks 2–3, 13 in week 4 and 8 in competition week. Part of this is a hard 28-day evidence-peak accessory branch; a reviewer should justify the abrupt change rather than assume the threshold proves fatigue management is optimal.

Running progresses 18→18.30→18.65 km, then 15.30 in the running deload, 3.15 in bench-meet week, and 2.80 training+10 race km in 10K week. Initial race-repeat work is 3.25–3.35 km, approximately 18% of training distance. Race repetitions use current 5:00/km fitness, not the 4:42/km aspiration; easy running is 5:54–7:00/km as a heuristic range. Both runs being near half the weekly total reflects limited frequency. The final 10K receives little running support for two weeks, and the two-run taper does not qualify for the three-run rhythm reminder. This may be an accepted consequence of the bench priority; it weakens the case for promising a faster 10K. A justified alternative for review is retain a little easy frequency and short current-fitness rhythm once meet recovery is known, rather than enforce a large generic cut or add missed mileage later.

In the synthetic completion replay, week 2 Monday bench feels one RPE harder with low energy; other days are normal, no pain. The engine remains on-track and does not alter the schedule. No review approves a change in eight weeks. Different heavy/volume rep schemes also restrict comparability in the positive top-set pathway. This demonstrates stability in this case; it does not prove all adaptation is stable or the meet outcome will improve.

## Athlete B — running-priority hybrid

Experienced runner, 40-minute 10K and 48 km/week, 16 km recent long run. Novice lifter with actual recent straight sets squat 70×6, bench 50×6 and deadlift 90×6 at RPE 6. Target 10K 31 January (week 12), 38-minute aspiration. Strength progression is explicitly requested, so this is not the engine's default maintenance-only running-priority case.

The calendar is Monday squat+bench, Tuesday quality run, Wednesday easy, Thursday deadlift+bench+easy, Sunday long run. Four runs and two strength visits occupy five days. Initial combined Thursday estimate 92–124 minutes fits the declared 180-minute cap. An athlete with a 60-minute Thursday limit would need a different arrangement; these results must not be generalized to that availability.

Strength starts with two six-rep sets per lift/exposure at RPE 6; from week 3 it uses three sets at RPE 7, with one set in strength-deload weeks 5 and 10. Bench frequency is two, squat/deadlift one each. Suggested loads are bench 35 kg in weeks 1–2 and 37.5 thereafter; squat 50→52.5, deadlift 62.5→67.5. These are substantially below the athlete's recent exact-setup sets at the same or lower reported RPE. The profile's simple rep-max estimate plus novice percentage multiplier does not preserve that effort relationship. One session's RPE may be inaccurate; nevertheless the conservative entry and missing reconciliation deserve review. Gradually finding the effort-compatible load is preferable to forcing a calculated kg regardless of warm-ups.

In the counterfactual replay B chooses familiar loads by technique/RPE and adds 2.5 kg every two weeks while prescribed effort remains comfortable. This is a stated synthetic scenario, not an engine-predicted gain. Despite completed straight sets, `reviewSessions()` returns 0 throughout eight weeks and no positive progression proposal appears. The confirmed review-coverage limitation is that novice main-set evidence has no top-set review route. It does not show the athlete is incapable of progressing through effort-based execution, nor justify simply treating every novice work set as a maximum.

Initial quality is 4 km threshold work (about 16–17 minutes) within 10.65 km; other runs 10.65, 10.70 and 16 km total 48. Work families change through pyramids, race repeats and ladders, but work remains 4 km in normal weeks. This is only 8.3% of distance initially and 6.6% at the 60.8 km week 11 peak. It is a low-intensity-volume, one-quality approach, not evidence that the athlete has a measured polarized distribution. Easy range 4:43–5:36/km; threshold roughly 4:01–4:11/km, current 10K 4:00/km, faster short repetitions use the current shorter-distance equivalent. The 3:48/km goal does not replace current fitness.

The forward-preview mileage series is 48, 49.45, 50.90, 41.75, 52.45, 54, 55.65, 45.65, 57.30, 59.05, 60.80, 13.60 training km; final week separately adds 10 race km. That last training cut is approximately 78% from week 11; a recovered experienced runner may prefer less reduction and more familiar rhythm. This is an evidence-supported taper-review concern, not a confirmed physiological defect. The quality cap may underchallenge an experienced runner; an alternative requires recent comparable sessions, fatigue tolerance and running priority rather than an automatic larger hard-distance percentage.

The advancing-clock replay is materially different from the first forward preview. No new performance test is supplied, so the 25 October result becomes older than 56 days: week 7 generated work distance is 0 instead of the forecast 4 km. Volume remains 55.65 km, now easy. The engine requests recent fitness evidence; the 56-day cliff is a product rule, not proof fitness disappeared. A frozen reviewed schedule is not automatically regenerated this way. Provenance and the athlete's approval determine which behavior applies.

## Athlete C — general hybrid

Intermediate lifter with declared squat 130/bench 100/deadlift 165 kg maxima, recent five-rep working evidence; 50-minute 10K and 28 km/week. No event date; goals are long-term strength and running development. The fixture uses an undated 10K development objective rather than the distinct health-only `fitness` option; choosing health-only intentionally caps running and removes quality in this implementation. Requested squat twice, bench twice, deadlift once and three runs.

Calendar: Monday squat+deadlift+bench, Wednesday quality, Thursday squat variation+easy run, Saturday bench variation, Sunday long easy run. Although three strength visits are available, the first squat, deadlift and bench exposures cluster on Monday; the other days carry secondary volume. Monday has nine main sets plus accessories, with an estimated 90–133-minute inclusive visit. Combined Thursday is 91–122 minutes. In weeks 6–9 and 11–12, the Monday hard limit still removes two optional sets each of lateral raises and biceps work (hammer curl, then preacher curl); main doses remain. These actual omissions appear in `timeAdaptation`, so generous declared windows do not mean an entirely unconstrained output. A smaller support dose or more even distribution is a reasonable alternative if fatigue, attendance or session duration deteriorates; the model's cyclic scheduling score does not establish that this concentration is optimal.

Weeks 1–4 main work is generally one five-rep top @7.5 plus two five-rep back-offs @7; secondary volume is similarly three sets, with variations. Bench and squat have six main/variation sets each, deadlift three: 15 primary sets per week. All strength sets including support total 46 initially. These counts are descriptive; exercises overlap and cannot be converted directly into validated per-muscle effective sets. There are no singles, which is coherent with no near-term maximal-strength event. Development uses triples @8 with triple back-offs @7 for the first exposures, while secondary volume remains fives; the exact competition-lift dose repeats in normal weeks 6, 7, 9, 11. Variation choice still changes after blocks. Progress through stable RPE-based practice is possible; automatic numeric overload is not guaranteed, and the programme should state how execution and periodic review establish progress.

Running goes 28→29.70 by week 3, drops 24.35 in week 4, grows to 35.45 by week 11, then 29.10 in week 12. Initial quality 4 km is 14.3% of distance; normal-week share declines with mileage, and every fourth week is easy-only. Easy range 5:54–7:00/km; threshold 4:58–5:08/km; race repeats center on current 5:00/km. A modest base/one-quality structure is plausible for sustainable development. Five-week strength and four-week running deloads mean some component is reduced in weeks 4, 5, 8, 10, 12. The staggered reductions could suit concurrent recovery or reduce productive exposure unnecessarily; neither conclusion is established from a calendar alone.

C's fictional weeks 3–4 include repeated lower-body one-RPE overshoots, poor sleep/energy and soreness, without pain. The week 3 review proposes fatigue adjustment; the week 4 pre-execution review repeats a fatigue proposal because no prior proposal was accepted. All schedules and histories remain unchanged by the review. The engine is not silently changing the programme. Repeated unapproved proposals should not be mistaken for multiple applied cuts. The workload branch can act before a 2–4-week reflection window, which is a separate stability issue for testing. The subjective pattern supports a review, not a diagnosis of overtraining syndrome.

## Fast and slow post-marathon recovery

A separate C sensitivity adds a 1 November marathon. Fast recovery is a dated 6 November `normal` report. Slow recovery is a dated 6 November `lower` report describing persistent heavy legs and uncomfortable easy running. These explicit synthetic inputs test contradictory branches; they are not universal recovery durations or clinical clearances.

Fast scenario: week 1 running resumes 28 km in Aerobic foundation because the dated recovery report resolves the marathon gate. Yet every lift is clipped to 2×2@5.5 within 14 days, with accessories removed. The final unconditional marathon pass overrides the earlier resolved-recovery decisions. This is a confirmed implementation contradiction. A normal report alone is still insufficient to guarantee readiness for heavy lifting; the correction should reconcile evidence and current warm-ups rather than replace the restriction with blanket full-load clearance.

Slow scenario: weeks 1–2 running is 11.2 and 16.8 km, easy. In week 3, beyond 21 days after the marathon, running resumes 29.7 km including quality despite the unresolved lower-body report. Squat/deadlift still carry recovery review and RPE 6.5/6 bounded work. The recovered/unrecovered meaning is inconsistent across sports; this is confirmed and feasible in the generated programme. Elapsed time must not clear persistent symptoms. Conversely, indefinite global restrictions based on an old resolved report would also undertrain the athlete.

## What the challenge establishes

The engine can produce coherent, feasible differentiated blocks and preserve athlete approval. It has reproducible temporal/record eligibility, recovery-consistency and approved-adjustment defects, alongside the novice review-coverage limitation. The fixtures also reveal plausible undertraining from conservative load suggestions, fixed quality limits and large event cuts, and potentially excessive accessory/session concentration for an athlete with poorer tolerance. Synthetic output cannot demonstrate actual injury, overtraining syndrome, strength gain or a race improvement. The smallest next steps are the register's bounded correctness repairs and reviewed coaching experiments, with no replacement engine or live programme changes.
