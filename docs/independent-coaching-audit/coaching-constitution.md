# Proposed LHRF coaching constitution

For Ella's review, 7 November 2026. **Proposal only: no rules in this document have been implemented or activated.** It defines decision responsibilities and evidence standards, not a replacement generator. Its 2–4-week review horizon is a practical governance default requested for this audit, not a biologically validated threshold.

## 1. State the purpose before prescribing

Agree the athlete's priorities, event dates, competition movements, strength development versus retention for each relevant lift, running goals, present training, equipment and complete time windows. Resolve conflicting goals openly. An athlete can choose a constrained compromise; do not label it maximal development or race readiness. Store the reason, evidence and review date for exceptional restrictions. Separate a recovery restriction from a lasting training preference.

## 2. Preserve a stable baseline

An approved programme is the baseline. Routine session feedback does not rewrite it. Keep comparable anchor sessions/exercises long enough to assess progress; vary supporting work when purpose, tolerance or engagement warrants. An easy session does not automatically earn more weight, and a hard session does not automatically demand a deload. Distinguish executing an existing progression rule from changing that rule, weekly structure, goal or dose.

Normal material programme changes require a clear athlete-readable rationale and explicit athlete approval. The proposal must show what changes, what is preserved, the affected future sessions, why this is expected to help, uncertainties, and when it will be reviewed. Approval applies to a specific proposal and programme version; it is not indefinite permission to make further cuts or increases. Rejection/deferment leaves the approved plan unchanged and must not generate repeated pressure to accept.

## 3. Use feedback at the appropriate time scale

| Time scale | Permitted interpretation and response | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| During/after a single session | Record actual load, repetitions, steps, effort, completion and context; adjust that session's execution using existing effort/technique stop rules; monitor an unusual result | A new chronic capacity, persistent under-recovery, need to rebuild the block |
| Weekly reflection | Compare planned/completed work, adherence, session quality, symptoms, sleep/recovery and schedule feasibility; identify an emerging pattern and missing information | A causal diagnosis from one score or a universal readiness threshold |
| Approximately 2–4 weeks of comparable evidence | Review persistent mismatch with objective performance, adherence, recovery and goals; propose a small targeted change when the combined case is persuasive | A mandatory waiting period, an automatic entitlement to progress, or proof of adaptation from self-report alone |
| Injury, significant pain/illness or other substantial safety concern | Stop or modify provoking work immediately and seek appropriate assessment; ask targeted questions without waiting for weekly review | A specific diagnosis, blanket removal of unaffected training, or a requirement to keep training until a full review window elapses |

The window is interpreted in elapsed dates and actual comparable exposures, not arbitrary epoch-week boundaries, calendar rows or numbers of sets from one workout. Sparse training may need longer; clear repeated performance failure or major disruption may justify earlier review. A safety signal can require prompt action without a longer trend. Single-session autonomy to stop is preserved; approval is not a barrier to ending unsafe work.

## 4. Assess evidence quality before quantity

Use only completed, correctly identified and relevant observations: exact exercise/setup/execution, working-set role, comparable dose, actual date, programme identity and context. Exclude future dates, stale or unrelated programmes, warm-ups, failed/optional sets and unreliable effort from capacity claims unless their separate purpose is explicit. Deduplicate exposures. Distinguish a missing report from a bad report and an unknown load from a prescribed zero. Approximate corrections retain original values and review provenance.

For strength, consider load/repetitions at similar effort, technique, consistency and experience. An estimated maximum is an estimate, not a tested maximum. For running, consider actual work and recovery laps, duration, effort and conditions; do not equate a faster downhill or treadmill session with improved outdoor race capacity. Retain multiple dated results where available and explain which evidence governs the current estimate. Never infer private device data from absence or general expectations.

## 5. Progress the intended stimulus

For maximal-strength goals, preserve appropriate practice of the target movement and enough sufficiently heavy exposure while managing total work. Hypertrophy volume, low-load speed practice and heavy singles have different purposes; do not count all sets as equal. Strength-specificity evidence does not require maximal attempts or a heavy single for every beginner. Moderate-load work can still increase strength.

Progress load, repetitions, working volume, quality or technical consistency in a planned, measurable way; not all need increase together. An initial static dose can be appropriate for familiarisation or maintenance. Continued static prescriptions need evidence that the desired outcome is being met or a stated reason for holding. Beginner straight sets must not be excluded from review merely because they are not top sets. Effort governs provisional kilogram suggestions; no percentage-to-RPE chart guarantees an individual's response.

For power, prescribe fast intent and adequate recovery, explain technical/velocity stop rules, and distinguish observed bar velocity from a subjective impression. A named speed session alone does not establish power development; speed work is optional when its marginal benefit is uncertain.

## 6. Build running capacity with explicit load accounting

Start from recent tolerable running and the goal. Preserve sufficient easy running and distinguish threshold/tempo, interval, race-rhythm and sprint purposes. Progress complete sessions, including warm-up, recovery and cool-down. Use current-fitness estimates and contextual effort; aspirational target pace requires a justified bridge and cannot establish current readiness. Longest-run progression needs recent long-run and consistency evidence.

An easy-majority distribution is a useful starting principle. Exactly 80% easy distance, a universal second quality day, and a fixed weekly growth percentage are not universal laws. Show hard-work distance and estimated time separately; an average percentage can obscure the concentration and cost of a single session. Caps can be provisional product bounds, but must expose curtailed stimulus and an individual-review path. Do not claim a requested mileage curve was delivered if the actual calendar is lower.

## 7. Coordinate training without assuming interference

Consider lower-body fatigue, close placement of demanding sessions, time for recovery and the athlete's principal goal. Do not remove useful strength or running solely because both sports occur frequently. Concurrent-training effects differ for maximal strength, hypertrophy and explosive strength, and depend on training status and the actual dose. Preserve unaffected work when reducing an affected area. Make a priority tradeoff explicit rather than automatically defending a long run or main lift at all costs.

Post-marathon return is individual and reviewable. Elapsed days alone neither establish readiness nor justify indefinite restriction. Reconcile the same current recovery report across strength, running, calendar guards and approval. Fast and slow recovery paths must both remain possible. Do not clear persistent fatigue merely because a timer expired or erase a credible resolution with a later unconditional rule. Returning to intensity and restoring full prior volume are separate decisions.

## 8. Deload and taper for a reason

Planned consolidation can be useful, but every fourth/fifth week is a convention to evaluate, not a universal requirement. Review actual fatigue and the block's progression. Taper towards a specific event by reducing unnecessary fatigue while retaining suitable familiar intensity/skill; exact cessation timing is individual and lift/event dependent. Do not add unfamiliar training or extra maximum attempts near competition. Treat adjacent events as one recovery problem with explicit priority and conditional race effort.

## 9. Fit the whole programme honestly

Respect confirmed equipment/setup, complete visit windows, event logistics and fixed athlete choices. Time fitting must preserve required stimulus or expose a precise, reviewable omission. Optional work is removed before useful main work where appropriate, but optional never means physiologically worthless. Unknown equipment is not permission to invent availability; missing required equipment can block a session. Proposed substitutions must preserve purpose and retain distinct histories/load conventions.

Any accepted dose change must update the executable steps/sets, distance, duration, pace, work totals, timing checks and visible explanation consistently. Recheck the final calendar for meaningful training as well as feasibility. An engine that rejects everything can satisfy safety tests while failing the athlete's objective; include useful-dose positive controls.

## 10. Make change reviewable and reversible

Each normal change records: the question, evidence interval and quality, plausible competing explanations, objective/adherence/recovery context, smallest justified action, expected benefit, athlete approval and a follow-up point. Do not impose numerical thresholds until counterexamples have been evaluated: a good day, one bad day, a dense short cluster, stale/future evidence, missing data, improved performance despite subjective fatigue, persistent poor performance without low scores, and deterioration after an earlier reduction.

Evaluate one major change at a time where feasible. If a reduction does not resolve the problem, reassess rather than stacking further reductions. No proposal alters completed prescriptions, original observations, records, live drafts or programme provenance. Future policy changes are versioned and opt-in; preserve an audit trail and a return path to the prior approved future schedule.

## 11. Measure outcomes and admit uncertainty

Tests establish implemented behaviour, not physiological efficacy. Assess meaningful performance, adherence, tolerability, athlete confidence and sustainability over time. Record supported improvements separately from defects and unresolved preferences. Communicate uncertainty and invite disagreement. There is no guarantee of injury prevention, a personal best or race readiness from a generated block.
