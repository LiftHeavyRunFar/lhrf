## What was independently reproduced

The eight tests cover twenty-four A/B/C counterfactuals: five trajectories plus paired-route controls for slow, worsening and partial recovery. The same first-fortnight lifting clip appears even after a normal report. Context-only lower concern ceases to restrict quality running after the date window; supplying paired structured evidence changes running output. A/C and novice B then differ in their later lifting treatment. Unknown post-event recovery also projects a return to quality without a new report. Positive controls establish that valid post-event/nonfuture dating is already required for the existing normal-report clearance and that all target-event identities remain intact; those protections must survive a future correction.

These observations support reconciling the meaning and scope of recovery evidence. They do not establish that every user reaches the problematic context-only route: the current UI normally supplies both fields. They also do not establish that the future proposed dose is safe or sufficient. The fixed 7 November clock and absence of later observations mean projected later weeks are conditional software output, not reports that a human remained symptomatic or recovered.

The source athletes retain the PR #128 contrasts: A prioritises advanced bench work with modest running, B prioritises running while learning lifting, and C seeks sustainable general development. Adding a fictional marathon to A is a stress test of the engine, not advice that its modest running base warranted attempting one. Completing all trajectories in software is not independent physiological replication.

## Remaining decisions before implementation

1. Agree the minimal shared meaning of a dated recovery review and how a report binds to an event, activity and programme version. Do not encode the five scope questions as automatic clearance scores.
2. Make an overdue review operationally useful: state the reason, next relevant visit/date and contact/reassessment path. Avoid both automatic clearance and a historical flag that can indefinitely suppress unrelated work. No-report cases still contain real uncertainty; software cannot guarantee recovery.
3. Evaluate higher-risk false decisions: partially recovered running/lifting, recurrent symptoms after an initial return, older resolved evidence plus newer worsening, separate long-run intolerance, and an imminent event. R1–R10 describe these future requirements honestly but do not test an implementation yet.
4. Preserve ordinary athlete execution choices and immediate stopping for pain. Objective performance/adherence should strengthen normal programme-change reasoning without vetoing pain, persistent poor well-being or a voluntary decision to reduce activity.
5. Compare exact future prescriptions before any material change: useful heavy/volume practice, actual running steps/dose, event preparation, availability and the athlete's priorities. Demonstrate archive/rollback and unchanged completed prescriptions/Garmin links in the eventual runtime PR.
