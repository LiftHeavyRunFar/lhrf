## Speed bench: discrepancy and decision

The confirmed 8 November prescription is **5 × 2 at 77.5 kg, ceiling 5.5**. Half of 154.45 kg is 77.225 kg, rounded to 77.5. This is a fixed percentage calculation, not evidence that fatigue requires 77.5 kg. The programme itself acknowledges that recent speed practice has not calibrated the wave. Relative to 1 November's completed 6 × 2 at 100 kg, this is a **22.5% load reduction**, **16.7% repetition reduction**, and **35.4% external-load-volume reduction** (1,200→775 kg). These describe dose, not physiological fatigue or training quality.

At 20 days before the bench competition, a large cut cannot be justified merely by naming a taper. A lighter technique day can be appropriate after hard Friday work, or for slower/unstable execution, soreness or systemic fatigue; those observations are not available. Nor does completing 100 kg at approximate RPE 5 prove it was fast, paused to competition standard or the optimal speed load. There is no measured velocity.

**Proposal for 8 November coaching review:** retain **5 × 2**, starting suggestion **95 kg**, ceiling **5.5**. Use the familiar competition setup and pause; rehearse commands only if already practised. Rest **90–120 seconds**, longer if necessary for quality. Warm-ups and the first double must be crisp and comfortable; reduce the load by an available 2.5–5 kg step, extend rest, or end the work if effort, speed or position deteriorates. Never add sets or load to reach the ceiling. The 95 kg choice is a transparent coaching buffer below recent 100 kg practice, **not a research-established optimum**. It retains fewer doubles and 20.8% less external-load volume than last Sunday while providing more load-specific practice than an unexplained 50% wave. Expected fatigue: low if the intended speed and ceiling are retained, but above the current 77.5 kg prescription. Confidence: **moderate that calibration should be reviewed; low-to-moderate in 95 kg for this athlete without Friday response/current status**. If recent bench fatigue or technique gives a reason for the lighter load, retaining or reducing 77.5 kg is defensible; the higher suggestion is not mandatory.

**15 November:** if it is confirmed as an existing speed slot and 8 November, intervening bench work and next-day response support it, repeat **5 × 2 at 95 kg, ceiling 5.5**; no wave-driven increase to 100 kg. If the saved slot has fewer sets for a justified taper, do not increase the set count to five without another review. Actual current before row is unavailable, so this is a target requiring attribution, not an exact owner delta.

Speed work offers repeatable technique and acceleration intent. Its easy doubles must not be counted as equivalent to productive RPE-7-plus volume work or heavy competition singles. A speed label does not replace specific high-force practice. Equally, making every bench exposure heavy would destroy the intended distribution and increase fatigue.
