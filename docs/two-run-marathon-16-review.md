# Sixteen-week two-run marathon: draft coaching review

This is an AI coaching review, not independent human approval. The pathway is a separate fixed catalogue option for established runners with an event about 16 weeks away. It is not a shortened or automatically entered part of the 24-week plan. The inputs are intentionally not a claim about Mark's present health, running base, marathon recovery, event date or available long-run window.

Entry assumptions are recent regular twice-weekly running, separate comfortable 45- and 100-minute easy outings, and a normal next-day response. The 4–6-week period and durations are proposed coaching assumptions, not validated thresholds, guarantees or an automated readiness test. If unknown, insufficient or following a recent marathon, the athlete should first establish recovery and a sustainable base and seek an individual event-timing review; do not catch up or jump ahead. Check event cutoff and plan fuelling/hydration practice according to actual needs.

The shorter run stays conversational; the long run progresses with three lighter weeks and a two-week taper before the event week. At 9 minutes/km the 150-minute peak is about 16.7 km; the 42.195 km race is much longer. Time-bounding avoids forcing slow runners to chase one distance, but this plan may be inadequate for an individual and does not guarantee a safe finish. Two runs concentrate the long outing and offer little supporting volume; no marathon-effort rehearsal is programmed. Review actual long-run recovery, time on feet, training background, route, fuel and event cutoff before participation. A third easy run might offer more support when the athlete can accommodate it, but is not silently added.

Strength can remain a priority: space hard lower-body sessions and the long run where feasible; alternatively consider maintaining rather than building lower-body work during demanding running weeks, keeping athlete-selected days and lifting priorities. The programme does not automatically alter strength doses or schedules. Existing conflict warnings do not establish physiological recovery. Pain and illness require separate assessment; missed sessions are never made up and do not themselves establish fatigue.

Evidence informs broad choices, not the precise 16-week table: van der Worp et al. (2020), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32421886/ reported associations of volume and longest run with marathon performance; Smyth and Lawlor (2021), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34651125/ observed performance associations with disciplined taper patterns. Neither study establishes the safety, efficacy or exact durations of this two-run plan. Individual response and injury evidence remain uncertain.

The available historical marathon-priority handout spans 17 weeks and assumed four weekly runs; its 40–50 km base was an assumption, so it cannot validate a current two-run scenario. The reproducible scenario uses a synthetic Monday 16 November 2026 start, Tuesday/Sunday runs and Sunday 7 March 2027 event to test a 16-week horizon and no Monday training; it does not assert Mark's event date, pace or present readiness.

Existing limitations: the native logger requires actual distance plus time for timed runs; FIT export of timed steps is unsupported; the displayed template event is not automatically aligned to a real race. Synthetic browser tests are not real-athlete, Pixel, Garmin or human coaching acceptance. Release remains separately gated.

## Event-date and timed-run integration boundaries

The setup accepts a Monday for the first programme week and an event weekday, but has no input for the actual race date. It displays the computed template marathon date in the preview. An athlete must compare it with the actual event: the app cannot identify an earlier, later or wrong-week race and does not prevent acceptance on that basis. Changing the first week or event weekday can produce a desired calendar date only if the whole 16-week schedule fits; it does not establish that starting then is appropriate. The scheduler rejects an event weekday preceding the final week's training day. It does not silently skip, shorten or alter weeks to match a race.

Timed workouts in this branch remain unsavable without a measured, positive distance and duration; users should not invent distance. Timed steps cannot currently be exported as Garmin FIT workouts, and no watch test has been performed. Draft #186 separately permits null distance, and #187 adds a possible-duplicate warning for the personal Garmin import pathway, but neither is integrated or validated with this stacked draft. #186 is based on #185, and #187 is based on #186; adopting them here requires a separate combined integration and regression of persistence, history, import matching and offline behaviour. #187 does not merge uncertain records, cannot detect all duplicates, and has not been tested against a real Garmin. Until the relevant fixes are integrated and accepted, these remain real workflow limitations rather than completed functionality.
